Working paper

Tariff Shocks, Household Exposure, and Aggregate Demand

September 2026

Tariffs affect aggregate demand through the joint distribution of household import exposure and spending responses. I derive a path-weighted sufficient statistic that decomposes the direct purchasing-power effect into average exposure and between- and within-group covariance terms. A stylized small-open-economy HANK model with non-homothetic baskets illustrates the mechanism. Under the benchmark tariff process, sorting amplifies the fixed-basket statistic by 7%, mainly across expenditure groups. In general equilibrium, the expenditure gradient drives the heterogeneous-basket output gap, which nearly vanishes when basket composition is fixed. Imported-input tariffs account for most of the activity contraction, while final-goods tariffs generate unequal cost-of-living effects.
